## 1. The AI assistant: what it does, and who builds it

### What it does

You hand the assistant a matter, by voice or text, in English or Spanish, and it runs that matter by email until it is done. For example: "Contact Carlos, ask for the updated proposal, set up a meeting to discuss it, and follow up until Friday. Keep me copied."

1. It takes the matter: it turns your words into a mandate (whom it may contact, what it may ask, which times it may offer, how often to follow up, until when), asks first if a detail is missing, and reads the mandate back. Nothing is sent until you confirm.
2. It writes the emails from its own address, which says it is your AI assistant. You get a copy of each email, or a daily summary. When you allow it for a matter, it sends from your connected Microsoft mailbox instead.
3. It handles the replies within the mandate. When someone promises something, it proposes a task with a reminder, which you confirm with one tap.
4. It follows up on the agreed schedule, and stops at the end date or when the contact asks.
5. It books the meeting: it checks your availability in Meeting Guard AI, including your Google and Outlook calendars as of your phone's last sync, offers two or three free times with the time zone, books the one the contact picks with its tasks and reminders, and sends the confirmation.
6. It stops and asks you whenever something is outside the mandate: money, contracts, a question it may not answer, someone new copied in, or a reply it cannot read with confidence. You get an approval card with the options.
7. It tells you how a matter stands when you ask by voice, and answers aloud; you can interrupt it.
8. It takes your changes: pause, resume, a new deadline, no more follow-ups, or cancel, by voice or text, read back before they apply.
9. It watches a thread you pick in your Microsoft mailbox and drafts replies for your approval.
10. It briefs you before the meetings it arranged and sends a morning digest: what moved, what needs you today, which follow-ups are due. On Android it appears in the full-screen morning summary; for WhatsApp users, in the daily agenda once Stage 3 is live.
11. It keeps a timeline of every matter: every email, approval and promise, and any failed send or booking, with retry or cancel.

What it never does: pay, accept a price or negotiate money; agree to or sign anything contractual; promise anything beyond the mandate; contact anyone outside the mandate without your approval; send from your mailbox unless you allowed it; say something is done before it really is. The AI only suggests; a separate check in our own code decides whether anything may be sent or booked, against the limits you confirmed. Confirming the mandate is your approval for what it lists, and commitments become tasks only when you confirm them, as your V2 rule asks.

Where you use it: in the app, where you talk to it, answer its approval cards and get its notifications on your phone; and on the web agenda, where you delegate by typing, answer approvals and read long email threads on a larger screen. It is sold as its own subscription, separate from Pro and Premium, and bought in the app through Google Play or the App Store; the web agenda reads the same plan. It is a new module beside the AI assistant already in the app, which stays in Free, Pro and Premium with the allowances you chose on November 5.

Not in this price, for a later phase: whole-inbox triage (it watches only the threads you pick); assistant workflows on WhatsApp; shopping, travel or restaurant booking; languages beyond English and Spanish; a continuous phone-call style voice (the prototype measures its cost per minute, then we quote it as an optional add-on); and sending from Gmail, which needs a Gmail connection, Google's approval and an independent security assessment repeated every year, paid to the assessor. Until then, Gmail users use the assistant's own address, which works for everyone from day one.
